# MBA research questionnaire — v0.3

**5 October 2026 | Draft for expert review and pilot testing**

Project: Artificial Intelligence Adoption and Operational Efficiency: An Empirical Study of Productivity, Cost Optimization and Managerial Decision-Making.

This instrument is researcher-developed. It is not yet validated or ready for main recruitment. The question IDs are intended for the dataset and do not need to appear on the participant form.

## Participant information and consent

This MBA study examines AI use and operational performance perceptions in selected Chennai service units. Please answer about one operational team or service-delivery unit that you supervise, using the preceding six months. We welcome units that do not use AI. There are no preferred answers. Do not disclose client information or confidential records.

Participation is voluntary. You may skip questions or leave before submission. Responses will be used for academic research and reported in aggregate. The researcher will use codes to manage unit duplicates and organisation grouping; where code linkages exist, responses are confidential rather than fully anonymous.

Before launch, insert actual researcher/institution/contact details, confirmed retention arrangements, the withdrawal procedure, and the pilot-measured completion-time estimate. Do not publish this draft consent section with these details missing.

C01. I have read the information and voluntarily agree to participate. Yes / No. If No, end.

## A. Eligibility and unit selection

Please select your primary supervised unit. If you supervise more than one, use the unit receiving the largest share of your supervisory time. Use this same unit throughout. Do not provide multiple responses about the same unit.

S01. Are you aged 18 or above? Yes / No. If No, end.

S02. Is the operational unit you will describe located within Chennai city? Yes / No / Unsure. No or Unsure: pause eligibility; do not include until boundary is established.

S03. Which activity best describes the unit's main work? Customer support / Transaction processing / Back-office service delivery / Other: describe. Other: review against scope before inclusion.

S03b. Does this unit operate as an IT/IT-enabled business service, with information technology central to delivery of the service? Yes / No / Unsure. No: out of scope. Unsure: review eligibility before inclusion. Merely using office software does not establish sector eligibility.

S04. Do you directly supervise or manage this unit? Yes / No. If No, end.

S05. Have you worked in this unit for at least six months? Yes / No. If No, end.

S06. Has this unit operated throughout the preceding six months? Yes / No / Unsure. No or Unsure: end or eligibility review, respectively.

S07. Can you report on the unit's operational work and whether AI is used? Yes / I can report operations but do not know its AI status / No. No: end. Unknown AI status: answer unit context and applicable outcomes, skip the AI-use profile, and keep adoption unclassified for association analysis.

## B. Unit context

B01. Role: Team lead / Supervisor / Manager / Other operational leadership role.

B02. Tenure in this unit: 6 to less than 12 months / 12 to less than 24 months / 24 to less than 60 months / 60 months or more.

B03. Approximate current unit headcount: 1–9 / 10–24 / 25–49 / 50–99 / 100 or more / Not known.

B04. Organisation code: researcher-assigned code. B05. Unit code: researcher-assigned code. These are administrative fields, not requests for public company or client names.

B06. How familiar are you with this unit's operating costs? No direct knowledge / Limited knowledge / Sufficient knowledge to assess cost efficiency. First two options: skip the cost domain and record missing by design.

## C. AI-use profile

For this study, AI refers to machine-learning or generative-AI systems used for predictions, classifications, recommendations, or content generation in operational work. Fixed spreadsheet formulas and simple rule-based automation do not count on their own. Use AI only when you reasonably know that the application has these capabilities; otherwise choose Not known.

A01. During the preceding six months, was AI used for this unit's operational work? Yes / No / Not known.

A02. Across the whole preceding six months, how frequently was AI used in the unit's operational work, on average? Never / Less than monthly / Monthly but less than weekly / Weekly but less than daily / Daily or almost daily / Not known. Consider the whole period, including months before use began or after use stopped.

A03. Across the whole preceding six months, approximately what proportion of completed operational tasks involved AI assistance? None / More than 0 but less than 25% / 25% to less than 50% / 50% to less than 75% / 75–100% / Not known. Use tasks, rather than the proportion of staff using AI, as your basis; if that estimate is not feasible, select Not known.

A04. Which best describes the unit's usual AI integration across that period? No operational AI use / Isolated discretionary use / Used in selected recurring workflows / Embedded in most relevant recurring workflows / Changed too much to identify a usual level / Not known. These categories describe integration, not product sophistication. A changing level is not an ordinal score.

For A01 = No, verify A02 = Never, A03 = None, A04 = No operational AI use; skip A05–A08. For A01 = Not known or an omitted/declined A01 answer, keep adoption unclassified and skip A02–A08; continue applicable outcomes. Contradictions require review, not silent zero recoding. An omitted/declined B06 answer does not establish cost knowledge: skip the cost domain and preserve the omission/decline reason.

A05. What applications were used? Select all: Response drafting or summarisation / Document classification or extraction / Workload forecasting / Scheduling or routing recommendations / Quality or exception detection / Other: describe / Not known.

A06. Give one tool/application example and its operational use, if known. Optional; do not disclose proprietary or client information.

A07. How long ago did operational AI use first begin in this unit? Less than 3 months / 3 to less than 6 months / 6 to less than 12 months / 12 months or more / Not known. This is time since first use, not necessarily continuous exposure.

A07b. Did operational AI use stop at any point during the preceding six months? No / Temporarily stopped and resumed / Stopped and has not resumed / Not known. Recent adopters and discontinued users remain eligible; exposure changes are described rather than interpreted as a full six months of continuous use.

A08. Is the reported AI use organisation-approved? Fully approved / Partly approved / Not approved / Not known / Prefer not to answer. Context only; not an eligibility test.

## D. Operational outcomes — all units answer

For each statement, assess the selected unit over the preceding six months, regardless of whether it used AI. Use: **1 Strongly disagree; 2 Disagree; 3 Neither agree nor disagree; 4 Agree; 5 Strongly agree; Not known; Not applicable.** Do not treat Not known as the middle response.

### Productivity

P01. The unit completed a high volume of service work per staff-hour worked.

P02. Routine service tasks were completed with limited staff effort per task.

P03. A large proportion of available staff working time was spent completing service tasks.

### Process efficiency

E01. Work moved between processing stages with few avoidable delays.

E02. Service tasks were completed within the expected turnaround time.

E03. Work rarely needed to be repeated because of processing errors.

### Cost optimization — only respondents with sufficient cost knowledge

K01. The total operating cost was controlled relative to the volume of service delivered.

K02. The unit avoided unnecessary spending on operational resources.

K03. The unit kept expenditure on correcting completed work to a minimum.

Consider the full operational effort, including checking, training, and technology expenses where relevant. Do not interpret time savings alone as verified cost savings. These items concern perceived cost efficiency, not accounting-based ROI.

### Managerial decision-making effectiveness

D01. Operational decisions were made in time to address the unit's needs.

D02. Operational decisions were based on information relevant to the problem being addressed.

D03. Operational decisions helped resolve the unit's work problems.

## E. Contextual observations — optional

X01. Which issues affected operations during the preceding six months? Select all: Demand changes / Staffing changes / Training needs / Technology downtime / Data-quality problems / Workflow redesign / Other / None known / Prefer not to answer. Contextual description only.

X02. For AI-using units: How much additional staff effort was needed to check AI outputs? None / A little / A moderate amount / A large amount / Not known. Do not subtract this response from outcome scores.

X03. Describe one operational benefit, difficulty, or unchanged outcome you consider relevant. You may describe experience unrelated to AI. Optional; avoid identifiable or confidential information.

## Researcher coding and scoring specification

Table A.1 describes analysis coding. Participant response labels must be mapped explicitly to the import codes in Appendix B; no automatic label mapping is performed.

**Table A.1. Questionnaire coding and analytical use**

| Fields | Coding/use |
|---|---|
| C01; S01–S07 | Eligibility and branching; preserve raw answers |
| B01–B03 | Context; categorical variables, not equal-interval numbers |
| B04–B05 | Coded organisation and unit identifiers; check duplicates/clustering |
| B06 | Cost-knowledge filter; insufficient knowledge creates missing cost items |
| A01 | Import text yes / no / not known; omitted/declined answers remain missing with their reason; do not use numeric 1/0 |
| A02 | Never=0 through Daily=4; ordinal primary adoption indicator |
| A03 | None=0 through 75–100%=4; ordinal secondary coverage indicator |
| A04 | No use=0 through Embedded=3; ordinal secondary integration indicator; Changed too much and Not known are separate non-scored categories |
| A05 | Separate binary fields for each choice; unknown separate |
| A06 | Review AI classification; unresolved ambiguous use remains unknown |
| A07, A07b, A08 | Contextual categories; no causal interpretation |
| P01–P03; E01–E03; K01–K03; D01–D03 | 1–5; unknown/not applicable missing with separate missing-reason code |
| X01–X03 | Context only; redact identifying text before reporting |

Do not sum A02–A04 into an adoption scale or report Cronbach's alpha for them: they are distinct adoption descriptors. If supported, mean each three-item outcome domain separately with all three responses present. Report domain-specific N. A two-of-three mean is a possible separately reviewed sensitivity analysis, not an automatic pipeline feature. High scores indicate more favourable perceived performance; no items require reverse coding. Do not generate an overall efficiency score without additional conceptual and measurement justification.

The importer retains eligible records with documented missing analytical answers, but missing eligibility cannot establish inclusion. Schema/metadata errors stop the import; invalid codes in applicable analytical fields exclude the row with an audit reason. Unknown-adoption and cost-knowledge routes are applied before numeric checks for their skipped fields. Deduplication retains the earliest eligible valid-coded organisation/unit report. Any reviewed correction is made in a coding copy with a dated log, preserving the original export.

## Item-development traceability

All statements above are original draft wording; none is claimed to reproduce a validated published scale. AI-use questions are informed at the concept level by the NIST taxonomy (Theofanos et al., 2024) and enterprise/workplace survey distinctions. Productivity relevance is informed by Brynjolfsson et al. (2025), but these perceptions are not their operational productivity measure. Cost and decision items require reviewer assessment for observability and coverage. Sources and full limitations appear in the main report.

## Specific pilot checks

Version 0.3 has undergone an internal wording and routing review only. No expert or participant has yet assessed it. Cost item K03 now addresses corrective expenditure rather than repeating unnecessary-spending wording; its distinction from process rework still requires review; productivity P03 concerns time utilisation, and decision D02 concerns the information basis. Reviewers must determine whether these fit the intended domains. Retain item-level analysis if a defensible domain score cannot be supported.

Confirm that respondents distinguish output/effort from turnaround and rework; understand 'unit'; can answer cost questions without guessing; understand operational decision effectiveness; correctly distinguish AI from fixed automation; and can apply the six-month period despite changing use. Inspect possible ceiling effects, overlapping productivity items, and whether three cost items measure the intended domain. Revise items before main collection if needed. A pilot is a feasibility check, not proof of construct validity.
