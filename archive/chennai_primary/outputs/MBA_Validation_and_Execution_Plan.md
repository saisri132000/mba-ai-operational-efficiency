## Status definitions

Drafted: an actual reviewable document exists. Design checked: internal logic has been reviewed; this is not empirical validation. Pending external review: reviewer/supervisor input needed. Pending empirical validation: actual pilot or main data needed. Blocked dependency: cannot complete that element without stated information.

## Design corrections made during this draft

- The conceptual arrow is treated as an association, not a demonstrated causal pathway.
- Non-users receive outcome questions they can answer; statements do not assume AI caused improvement.
- Frequency is the proposed primary adoption indicator. Coverage and integration remain separate secondary indicators, not a supposedly validated scale.
- Cost knowledge is screened; unknown responses are missing rather than neutral or zero.
- Multiple operational units in one organisation are recognised as potentially dependent observations.
- No historical aspirational response range is adopted; the illustrative power calculation does not establish a sample requirement.
- Developed report sections and software are distinguished from empirical completion.

## Execution gates

### Gate 1 — Supervisor and access preparation

Confirm the institution's report template, chapter requirements, ethics process, permitted recruitment methods, and submission deadline. Identify reachable organisations without sending invitations yet. Record counts of eligible units rather than merely counting contacts. Establish whether a researcher-assigned organisation/unit coding system is workable.

Pass condition: scope accepted for planning, access estimate documented, and institutional requirements recorded. The writing draft remains usable even while these details are unknown.

### Gate 2 — Expert review and cognitive interviews

Obtain methods and operations feedback. Use the question IDs to record clarity, relevance, duplication, observability, and proposed edits. Conduct planned cognitive interviews with eligible people. Specifically review cost items and the geographic boundary. Do not describe suggested reviewers as actual reviewers.

Pass condition: important ambiguity resolved, item rationale recorded, consent details completed, and no question forces respondents to guess confidential information.

### Gate 3 — Pilot and protocol freeze

Test branching, completion burden, missingness, response ranges, and coding. Keep pilot data separate. Revise and retest changed routes. Finalise sample justification and collection rules before inspecting main outcomes. Freeze the instrument and analysis protocol with a date/version.

Pass condition: usable instrument and documented recruitment/analysis rules. Pilot estimates are not claimed to establish full validity.

### Gate 4 — Main collection

Recruit under the confirmed permissions and protocol. Preserve the invitation log and original export. Monitor eligibility, duplicate units, organisation concentration, and recruitment reach. Do not monitor p-values as a stopping rule. Invitations and messages to others require the user's explicit instruction before this assistant sends them.

Pass condition: collection completed under the documented rule; actual achieved sample and deviations recorded.

### Gate 5 — Analysis and Chapters 4–5

Clean and analyse genuine data with an audit trail. Assess whether measurement and organisation spread support each planned analysis. Report weak measurement, extensive missingness, or insufficient variation rather than hide it. Populate results and discussion only after checks.

Pass condition: reproducible results, bounded interpretation, supervisor-required formatting, and final review.

## Access log fields

Organisation code; recruitment route; eligible unit estimate; permissions needed; manager/team-lead contact count; invited count; screened count; eligible count; submitted count; duplicate count; geographic uncertainty; notes. Store contact information separately from the analytical dataset. A response rate is calculable only where the invitation denominator is known; do not invent a denominator for open links or referrals.

## Review log fields

Date; document/version; question or section ID; issue; evidence; reviewer role; decision; revision; status; next check. No reviewer names or decisions have yet been entered.

## Results tables to populate later

1. Recruitment and eligibility flow — actual invitations where known, submissions, eligibility exclusions, duplicates, retained units.
2. Sample composition — organisations, operational activities, role, tenure, headcount categories.
3. Adoption profile — frequency, coverage, integration, applications, adoption duration, unknown status.
4. Outcome items — distribution and missingness, with analysis-specific denominators.
5. Measurement assessment — domain evidence and scoring decisions, where supported.
6. Primary associations — estimate, uncertainty interval, raw and Holm-adjusted p-value where defensible, domain N, dependence handling.
7. Sensitivity/exploratory results — alternative missingness scoring, recent-adopter handling, secondary adoption indicators, organisation concentration.

These proposed tables have no invented entries. Generated reports presently show item counts/medians, conditional associations, and an adoption-frequency chart; distributions and alpha diagnostics remain in private analysis output. Contextual/recruitment profiles, sensitivity results, intervals, and any cluster analysis need separately reviewed methods and real inputs. Findings, operational recommendations, and the final empirical abstract must await actual evidence.
