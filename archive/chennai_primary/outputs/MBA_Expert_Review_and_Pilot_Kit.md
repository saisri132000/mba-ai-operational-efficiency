# Expert review and pilot kit

**1 October 2026 | Questionnaire v0.3 | No reviews or pilot responses collected**

## Purpose

Use this kit to evaluate the questionnaire before main recruitment. The instrument measures reported AI use and perceived current performance, not respondents' estimates of AI-caused improvement. Both users and non-users must be able to answer the outcome items. Expert review, cognitive interviews, and feasibility piloting provide different evidence; none by itself proves full validity.

## 2. Expert-review instructions

Seek two or three reviewers covering service operations and research methods, including operational-cost knowledge. This is a planned review, not a completed review. Provide the chapter draft and questionnaire together so reviewers can assess construct definitions as well as individual questions.

For each item, score clarity and relevance separately: 1 = major problem; 2 = needs substantial revision; 3 = minor revision; 4 = suitable as written. Record the reason, not merely a score. No passing average automatically validates an item.

Reviewers should examine the following domain questions:

- Is AI use distinguishable from fixed automation without requiring technical expertise?
- Do frequency, coverage, and integration refer to the same period and unit?
- Does productivity measure output/effort rather than only speed or busyness?
- Does process efficiency cover timeliness and rework without duplicating productivity?
- Can managers actually assess resource-cost efficiency? Does K03 assess corrective expenditure without simply duplicating process rework?
- Does decision effectiveness cover outcomes sufficiently, or primarily information inputs?
- Can units with different task complexity interpret the wording comparably?
- Do non-users and unknown-status respondents receive appropriate routes?

Record reviewer role, review date, version, conflicts or limitations, and every resulting decision. Do not claim that a published source validates the original wording.

## 3. Cognitive-interview guide

Provisionally invite five to eight eligible respondents after actual consent information and applicable requirements are established. Seek different operational activities, AI users and non-users, and differing cost knowledge. This is a usability sample, not a representative sample.

Ask the person to answer while explaining their reasoning. Avoid coaching them toward a preferred response. Useful prompts:

1. Which specific unit did you select, and why?
2. What did you count as AI? What would you exclude?
3. How did you estimate frequency over six months if use changed?
4. How did you estimate the proportion of tasks involving AI?
5. What does 'staff-hour worked' mean in your unit?
6. How do you distinguish productivity from turnaround and rework?
7. What information supports your cost answer? Would 'not known' be more accurate?
8. Describe the type of operational decision you considered.
9. When would you choose the midpoint, Not known, or Not applicable?
10. Did any question feel sensitive, repetitive, or impossible to answer?

Record interpretation, evidence used, difficulty, proposed revision, and duration. Do not retain confidential examples or identifying client details. After interviews, revise unclear items and record the reasons. A small interview count does not establish saturation or psychometric validity.

## 4. Survey routing checks

Run these cases through the actual survey platform before any pilot. They are test scenarios, not research participants, and must never enter the research dataset.

**Table E.3. Instrument-review specification**

| Scenario | Expected route |
|---|---|
| Consent declined | End; no substantive research questions |
| Under 18 or no direct supervisory responsibility | End |
| Location, sector, or activity uncertain | Eligibility review; exclude until resolved |
| Eligible unit with no AI use | Zero-use indicators confirmed; skip AI details; answer outcomes |
| Eligible unit with unknown AI status | Adoption unknown; outcome descriptions only |
| AI use with insufficient cost knowledge | AI profile and other outcomes; skip cost domain |
| First use within six months | Whole-period frequency; recent exposure recorded |
| AI use discontinued | Remain eligible; stopped-use status recorded |
| Integration changed too much | No ordinal integration score; other valid fields retained |
| Optional item skipped | Valid missing value, never automatic zero or midpoint |
| Duplicate report for same unit | Flag; apply documented duplicate rule |

Proposed duplicate rule: retain the first eligible submitted unit report; later submissions do not replace it based on more favourable outcomes. If the first report is unusable, document the reason before retaining the next eligible report. Organisation/unit codes must work before launch; otherwise redesign duplicate controls.

## 5. Feasibility-pilot summary

Provisionally collect 15–25 eligible pilot responses if accessible. Keep them separate from main data. Record:

- Invitations where the denominator is known, completions, and eligibility outcomes.
- Completion time distribution and comments on burden.
- Unknown/not-applicable frequency per item and the cost-eligible count.
- Distribution of adoption categories and each outcome item.
- Routing faults, unresolved AI classifications, and duplicate reports.
- Whether item wording produces misunderstanding or markedly concentrated answers.

Review every routing failure before launch. Extensive unknown cost answers may require recruiting more cost-informed respondents, narrowing the cost question, or treating the domain as a secondary descriptive outcome. Changing its role must happen before main analysis and be reflected in the title/objectives if material. Pilot reliability estimates, if calculated, are preliminary and unstable; do not use a small pilot to claim construct validity or a dependable expected association.

## 6. Release decision record

Complete: version; reviewers; interviews completed; pilot N; unresolved issues; changes; consent details; sample justification; collection window; stopping rule; organisation-dependence approach; estimator and interval method; release date.

Release only after critical wording/routing issues are resolved and institutional requirements satisfied. No release date is currently assigned. Main-data measurements may still fail to support intended scores after a successful usability pilot.
